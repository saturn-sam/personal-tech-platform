professionalSummary:
  title: Professional Summary
  intro: A documentation-first engineering practice shaped by platform operations, infrastructure architecture, and long-term maintainability.
  paragraphs:
    - I work on enterprise infrastructure with an emphasis on repeatable platform operations, resilient delivery paths, and operational clarity. The work that matters most to me sits at the boundary between architecture and execution, where design choices have to survive real operational pressure instead of looking good only in diagrams.
    - My strongest areas are Kubernetes platforms, virtualization, GitOps-oriented workflows, observability, infrastructure governance, and documentation systems that help teams move from tribal knowledge to shared operational practice. I care about decisions that reduce toil, improve recovery, and make engineering systems easier to reason about over time.
    - PTKP exists as an extension of that practice. It is both a public knowledge platform and a disciplined way to convert projects, certifications, architecture work, and technical notes into reusable engineering assets.

personalStory:
  title: Personal Story
  intro: The path here has been built through steady technical curiosity, hands-on infrastructure work, and a growing commitment to knowledge sharing.
  paragraphs:
    - 'My interest in technology started with the practical side of systems: understanding how machines, networks, and software fit together, and why small configuration decisions can create very large outcomes. That curiosity naturally moved from isolated tools into complete environments, where infrastructure had to be stable, observable, and maintainable.'
    - Over time, the focus shifted from learning individual technologies to understanding systems as connected platforms. Virtualization, operating systems, networking, automation, cloud services, and container orchestration stopped being separate subjects and became parts of the same operational picture.
    - Early professional experience reinforced the value of clear process, reproducible implementation, and technical writing. The more complex the platform became, the more important it was to document not only what was built, but why it was built, how it should be operated, and what tradeoffs were accepted.
    - Today, that same perspective shapes both engineering work and PTKP itself. The long-term goal is to keep building practical platforms while also producing durable knowledge that helps other engineers move faster with better context.

learningJourney:
  title: Learning Journey
  intro: Continuous learning is part of the engineering model, not a separate activity.
  paragraphs:
    - I learn best by building, documenting, and revisiting systems over time. That means combining formal study with lab work, implementation notes, architecture comparisons, and operational retrospectives. When a concept is important, I want to understand how it behaves under constraints, not just how it appears in a product overview.
    - Certifications help validate structured knowledge, but they are only one part of the process. Self-study, reading, testing, troubleshooting, and rewriting explanations in my own words are equally important. PTKP is intentionally designed to preserve that process so learning becomes reusable for future work.
    - Knowledge sharing is also part of learning. Explaining a pattern clearly enough for another engineer to adopt it is one of the most reliable ways to test whether the understanding is real or still incomplete.

philosophy:
  title: Philosophy
  intro: Engineering work should be understandable, durable, and operationally honest.
  paragraphs:
    - I prefer systems that are clear over systems that are merely impressive. A design should make responsibilities visible, reduce ambiguity for operators, and stay understandable when the original implementer is no longer in the room.
    - 'Leadership in engineering is often expressed through clarity: clearer requirements, clearer documentation, clearer interfaces, and clearer expectations during failure. A team moves faster when operational knowledge is shared instead of concentrated.'
    - Problem solving starts with constraint recognition. I want to understand what is fixed, what is flexible, and what tradeoff is actually being made. Many technical problems become easier once the constraints are described accurately.
    - Learning should produce artifacts. Notes, architecture records, reference checklists, and implementation guides are not side effects; they are part of the engineering outcome because they extend the value of the original work.
