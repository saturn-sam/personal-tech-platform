Enterprise Kubernetes architecture should make operational boundaries visible before workload teams depend on the cluster. The platform is easier to maintain when control plane ownership, worker placement, networking, storage, and observability are documented as one operating model.

<Callout type="important" title="Operating principle">
  Design the cluster so ownership, failure domains, and recovery actions are clear during incidents,
  not only during initial deployment.
</Callout>

## Control Plane Boundaries

The control plane should remain isolated from tenant workloads and routine platform services. Administrative access, certificate rotation, backup posture, and audit logging need explicit ownership because failures in this layer affect every workload on the platform.

## Worker Segmentation

Worker pools should reflect workload risk and operational behavior. Separating infrastructure services, application workloads, and specialized compute nodes reduces noisy neighbor risk and simplifies maintenance windows.

```yaml
apiVersion: v1
kind: Namespace
metadata:
  name: platform-observability
  labels:
    ownership.ptkp.io/team: platform
    workload.ptkp.io/class: infrastructure
```

## Operational Ownership

The operating model should define who responds to platform alerts, who approves cluster changes, and which recovery steps are safe to run during an incident. Architecture documents become useful only when they connect design decisions to repeatable operations.
