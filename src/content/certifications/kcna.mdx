## Overview

The **Kubernetes and Cloud Native Associate (KCNA)** certification, offered by the Cloud Native Computing Foundation (CNCF), validates foundational knowledge of Kubernetes and the broader cloud-native ecosystem. It covers the core concepts required to understand how modern applications are designed, deployed, and managed using containers, Kubernetes, and cloud-native technologies.

KCNA serves as an excellent foundation for engineers beginning their Kubernetes journey while also providing a strong conceptual framework for more advanced certifications such as CKA, CKAD, and CKS.

## Exam Scope

- Kubernetes architecture and core concepts
- Container fundamentals
- Pod lifecycle and workloads
- Kubernetes networking basics
- Storage concepts
- Service discovery and ingress
- Cloud-native architecture
- Observability and monitoring
- Kubernetes security fundamentals
- CNCF landscape and ecosystem
- GitOps concepts
- DevOps and platform engineering principles

## Study Approach

Preparation focused on building a strong conceptual understanding of Kubernetes and cloud-native computing through the official Kubernetes documentation, CNCF learning resources, Linux Foundation training, and extensive hands-on practice. Rather than relying solely on theory, I reinforced each topic by deploying Kubernetes clusters, exploring workloads, networking, storage, and observability components in laboratory environments.

This certification established the foundation that supported my progression toward advanced Kubernetes certifications, including CKA, CKAD, CKS, KCSA, and ultimately the Kubestronaut designation.

## Practical Value

KCNA provides the essential knowledge required to understand how modern cloud-native platforms are designed and operated. Although it is an entry-level certification, the concepts it covers form the basis for enterprise Kubernetes deployments, platform engineering, and cloud-native application development.

The knowledge gained through KCNA contributes to my work in designing and operating enterprise infrastructure, VMware Tanzu environments, Kubernetes platforms, automation, observability, and DevSecOps practices. It also serves as the conceptual foundation for documenting architecture, troubleshooting production environments, and sharing practical engineering knowledge through this platform.
